The One-Man Kitchen

From Clinton House to a triangle diner

Jeff Golden started at sixteen, apprenticing at the Clinton House — the kitchen legend says birthed Utica Chicken Riggies. He ran kitchens for hundreds at Turning Stone Casino and Hamilton College, then walked away from corporate food.

He bought the old Triangle Coffee space and opened Golden's. No weekends. No takeout. Just sixteen seats and a guy who cooks everything himself, from scratch, while you watch.

"I'm looking for quality over quantity. And it seems to work."

Come Hungry

244 Genesee Street, Utica

Monday through Friday, seven to two. Closed weekends. No takeout — the food doesn't travel well, and Jeff doesn't want it to.

You'll sit at the counter. You'll watch the eggs hit the griddle. You might hear a story about the '80s kitchen scene, or why he forages ramps for the tomato salad.

It's not a restaurant. It's a conversation with breakfast.

"I'm pretty much a bartender with food."
